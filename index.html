Algorithms for Decision Making - Companion

Concepts, pictures, and code for Algorithms for Decision Making

This site is an independent, unofficial companion to Algorithms for Decision Making by Mykel Kochenderfer, Tim Wheeler, and Kyle Wray (MIT Press, 2022), the textbook for Stanford AA228/CS238. The book is freely available at algorithmsbook.com.

Every chapter has a page with the big picture, intuition, the key equations explained term by term, worked examples with real numbers, interactive figures, tested code in Python and Julia, and solved problems.

The book in one picture

The book is about one agent, or several, choosing actions under uncertainty. Part I builds the tools: probability to describe what we don’t know, and utility to say what we want. Parts II–V then each add one source of uncertainty (Figure 1): random outcomes of actions over time, an unknown model, a hidden state, and other agents. Within a Part, the chapters typically move from stating the problem, to solving it exactly, to approximating when exact is too expensive.

How to use this site

Each chapter page is laid out the same way:

  1. Big picture: where the chapter fits, its key ideas, and its vocabulary.
  2. Intuition: a concrete story before any math.
  3. Formalism: the definitions and key equations, in the book’s notation, each explained in words and term by term.
  4. Worked example: the ideas applied to real numbers.
  5. Interactive: figures you can change.
  6. Algorithms & code: implementations in Python and Julia, side by side.
  7. Solved problems: try each one before opening its solution.
  8. Recap and Cheat sheet & connections: the key points, the key equations, and links to related chapters.

Big picture and Intuition are written to stand alone: reading just those two sections of every chapter gives a tour of the whole book.

Each page states which language was executed to produce its figures. The code in the other language is tested separately.

Three reference pages collect material from across the site:

  • Notation: every symbol, grouped by Part.
  • Cheat sheets: every chapter’s key equations in one place.
  • Problems: the gallery of problems, with loading code in both languages.
